# imabubakr.github.io

Personal site of Muhammad Abu Bakar (Geospatial Data Specialist, Freiburg).
One page per language: English at `/` (`index.html`), German at `/de/` (`de/index.html`).

## Deploy and run

- GitHub Pages, classic mode: the root of `main` is published as-is (`.nojekyll`, no build step).
  Pushing to `main` puts changes live at https://imabubakr.github.io/.
- Local preview: `python3 -m http.server 8000` in the repo root, then open `localhost:8000/` and `localhost:8000/de/`.
  It sends no `Cache-Control`, so browsers may run stale JS/CSS: hard-reload. CSS/JS links carry `?v=YYYYMMDDx`;
  bump it in `index.html`, `de/index.html` (and `404.html` for CSS) whenever those files change.
- Old URLs are redirect stubs and must keep working: `about.html` → `/#about`; `resume.html`, `contact.html`,
  `projects.html` → `/#cv`; `geo.html` → `/`. Keep section ids (`about`, `cv`, `contact`) stable; both languages share them.

## Hero map pipeline

1. `cd tools/contours && npm ci` (once). Needs GDAL ≥ 3.6 with Python bindings and numpy.
2. `tools/contours/make_contours.sh` downloads Copernicus GLO-30 around 47.997791 N, 7.842609 E, reprojects to
   WGS 84 / UTM 32N, smooths, runs `gdal_contour` (10 m, every 5th = index), simplifies with mapshaper, places
   uphill-facing labels, runs SVGO. See `--help` for options (`--dem` for a local DEM).
3. `python3 tools/contours/build_hero.py index.html de/index.html` injects the map, graticule ticks, coordinates and
   the DEM credit between the `<!-- build:name -->` markers, formatted per `<html lang>`.
   `make_contours.sh` also writes the 50 m elevation grid and the map→lat/lon formula used by `hero-map.js`.
   Region map for the fullscreen viewer (Kaiserstuhl to Feldberg, 40 x 36 km, CI 20 m, 100 m grid): run the
   region command from `make_contours.sh --help` (own `--work` folder!), then
   `python3 tools/contours/region_extras.py` (summit labels found as DEM maxima, hero frame and marker).
4. If the hero changed visibly, regenerate `assets/img/og.png` with `tools/og/make_og.mjs` (instructions in the file).

## Plates (Visualizations section) and certificates

Edit the `PLATES` or `CERTS` list (source file, EN/DE short title and caption) in `tools/plates/build_plates.py`, then run
`python3 tools/plates/build_plates.py` (needs Pillow and ffmpeg; `--skip-media` to only rewrite the markup).
**The first plates are rendered videos of Freudenberg (Siegerland, NRW), made with `tools/media/` (see its README):**
a classified LiDAR fly-around (NRW point clouds only separate ground/non-ground, so buildings = inside ALKIS
footprints and > 1.5 m above ground, vegetation = other non-ground > 0.5 m; three.js in headless Chromium,
1280 px, fixed bitrate: point clouds are ~90 MB at CRF settings) and a DOP10 orthophoto with the ALKIS building
footprints drawing in. Data: Geobasis NRW, dl-de/zero-2-0 (credited in the captions). The building-outline plate (`make_ortho.py --errors`) shows simulated, slightly wrong outlines in cyan, close
to the roofs (owner's wish; fine-tuning a real model was started and stopped at his request); its caption says "simulated". The LiDAR plate caption is exactly the owner's sentence. Captions must stay truthful: the building footprints are official ALKIS data, not model output (the owner
asked; a false claim would be detectable and risky in applications). They are listed in `PLATES`
with `src=None` (pre-rendered, skipped by `build_media`).
Plate 04 is a landslide MODEL SCENARIO (`tools/media/landslide.py`, see its docstring): 2 m DTM from the Freudenberg
LiDAR ground points; steady-state hydrology m = min(1, q·a/(T·sinβ)) with MFD catchment area (hollows saturate first);
infinite-slope FS; the slide = compact FS<1 cluster at peak rain (150 mm/d, an extreme event); volume/mass from area
× 1.5 m; runout = Voellmy mass point on MFD paths (μ 0.3, ξ 300) with velocity and arrival time, cross-checked with
Corominas (1996); deposit volume-conserving. Rendered in 3D (three.js, `landslide3d.html`): DOP10 orthophoto as ground texture, sun with soft shadows,
rain streaks and a darker sky during the storm, soil water in blue, ~6000 debris particles on Voellmy trajectories,
a push-in and slight shake at the failure, FS colours fade after the slide; the scar sinks (×3) and the deposit
builds up (×1.5) in the mesh. Encoded at a fixed bitrate (rain is expensive), ~5 MB. Results in work/landslide.json and in the caption, which says it is a scenario with assumed values.
Added 2026-10-10 from the owner's Downloads: vehicle detection video (pre-rendered, `src=None`; source stays in
~/Downloads, not in the repo), tree crowns (`tree-crowns.png`), population density Europe (`population-europe.png`,
cropped from a geemap screenshot to remove the UI; basemap credit in the caption). Captions neutral until the owner
confirms sources/methods.
**Selection (2026-10-10):** all plots were listed, then the owner removed 50 in three rounds; 36 plates remain (6 of them
animations), titled from what is visible (no invented facts; proper captions still to do). Never on the page:

web.jpg, databases.jpg, prog4.gif/sen1.gif (copies of home1.gif). The preview is sticky beside the list. Video settings: very light denoise, VP9 CRF 36 / x264 CRF 27;
line-art animations in `UPSCALE` get 2x Lanczos (Storm Emir looked blotchy with the old hqdn3d 3 / CRF 40), and
`STILLS` gives Storm Emir a sharper preview frame (0055.png).
It writes the media to `assets/plates/` and the plate index / certificate grid into both pages between

## "From orbit to drone" (career story)

Section [02] tells the owner's path: satellite data (from 2016) → aerial imagery (from 2021, MapTransfer) → drones
(since 11/2025, Beagle Systems). Chapter texts (owner's brief, 2026-10-10): title, then the platforms / sensors worked
with (from the CV, no project or employer names), then one sentence on what that resolution shows. The owner
confirmed the sensor lists as complete (2026-10-10): satellite = Landsat 7/8, Sentinel-1/-2, MODIS, ASTER, TRMM;
aerial = orthophotos; drone = cameras and LiDAR. Do not add sensors. Three chapters (`.ds-ch`, EN/DE text in the HTML) scroll past on the left while the
**page background itself** (`backdrop.js`) does the descent on the right (owner's pick, 2026-10-09: "you nailed it"):

- Satellite and aerial stops made livelier (owner, 2026-10-10: "boring there", the drone view is perfect):
  chapter 1 shows a **Sentinel-2 pass**: a descending pass (north to south) along the ground track, timed so the
  satellite is mid-screen while the chapter is centred and over Freiburg as the zoom begins; the swath fills in
  behind a scan line with a small satellite symbol, the swath ahead is faint. (Other sensors' swath edges,
  Landsat / Sentinel-1 / MODIS, were tried and removed by the owner: just the sweep.) Chapter 2 shows an **aerial survey being flown**: the camera hovers
  (`hold`: the route position stops at `SURVEY_AT` = 6900 m, over Wiehre, and the flight before/after is
  stretched to fit) while the aircraft flies 3 strips of 5 frames (0.6 x 0.4 km, illustrative; 25 % forward
  and side overlap, the owner's figure; `SV`) with turns between them. The aircraft is a navigation-display
  chevron (the owner disliked a plane icon), with a solid line for the track flown over the dashed plan. The
  frames taken so far form one seamless mosaic (one fill, no seams or frame outlines: owner), only the frame
  being taken is outlined and labelled. The mosaic covers the terrain lines (paper fill)
  and show a small map of what the photo sees (owner: "add roads or a few more features, so it becomes more
  clear"): woods (faint tint), water, streams and the Dreisam in `--water`, footpaths dashed, streets, main roads
  as cased double lines, tram, railway with cross ticks, buildings lightly filled (OpenStreetMap,
  `assets/topo/aerial-features.json`, ~70 KB gzipped, from `tools/orbit/build_aerial.py`; layer styles in
  `FEAT_STYLE`). Performance: drawing all features live cost up to 40 ms a frame (the camera is usually still
  easing during the survey), so they are painted into an offscreen canvas and each frame draws it with an
  affine transform fitted to three ground points (looking straight down a camera move is ~a shift and scale);
  it is repainted only when zoom or tilt drift too far or the camera rests (~8 repaints per pass). The affine
  shortcut is wrong once the camera tilts, so the whole survey (map, mosaic, track, frame) fades out between
  87° and 80° pitch, with no repaint during the tilt (the owner saw the map warp and snap there). Orbit scroll
  then costs the same as before the survey existed (~20 ms/frame in headless Chromium). Footer credits OpenStreetMap (ODbL). Not built: the 30 m "handful of squares"
  inset and a tilted aerial view (offered, not chosen).
- Keyframes sit on the chapters' real positions (`layoutKeys`): climb to orbit for chapter 1 (705 km, north-up so
  Europe reads the right way: 5° graticule, limb, country outlines only (Natural Earth coastlines + land borders,
  solid, no rivers or small islands: owner's wish, 2026-10-09),
  a Sentinel-2 swath of 290 km along a ground track ~13° east of north through Freiburg, Freiburg marked in accent),
  turn back to the route heading on the way down, look straight down from 1 km for chapter 2 (overlapping aerial
  photo frames, now the survey described above, labelled "AERIAL PHOTO · 20 CM"), then tilt
  forward at 100 m for chapter 3 (a camera viewfinder: corner brackets + centre cross, "DRONE CAMERA · ~2 CM"),
  and level out to the normal flight after the section.
- The zoom into Freiburg (30 km → 6 km) is tilted to ~60° with relief close to real (keyframe column 7 = factor on EX:
  0.58 at 30 km and 6 km, i.e. ~1.5× real, 0.7 at the 1 km aerial stop, 1 by drone height; `ex = EX × factor`).
  The owner found ×4, ×2 and ×1.4 boosts all too exaggerated. Country outlines fade out above 60 km (they are not draped on the terrain).
- Looking down (pitch > 45°) the ridge-line spacing follows the screen (a line every ~6.5 px, power-of-2 steps
  of 150 m so lines stay fixed; thinner strokes when dense): the joy plot stays dense from 30 km down to 1 km
  (owner's wish). Level flight keeps the height-based spacing.
- Smoothness (owner felt jerkiness, 2026-10-11): frame rates were fine; the jerk was the camera height jumping up to
  7 m whenever a new hilltop entered the 6 km look-ahead window. The ground profile along the route is now
  precomputed (20 m steps, 50 m look-ahead sampling) and smoothed over ~1 km (`buildGround`), so the camera glides.
  Adaptive quality only coarsens sample spacing, never the number of ridge lines (lines popped in and out).
- Performance (owner noticed lag, 2026-10-10): static sphere geometry (graticule, outlines, swath) is converted to
  scene points once (`toScene`), theme colours are cached (`readColors` on theme change), style writes only on change,
  camera easing 0.2 s, and adaptive quality while moving (`q` 1–2.5 scales sample spacing and line density when a
  draw takes > 7 ms; a full-quality frame when the camera rests). Benchmark with Playwright + CDP CPU throttling and
  `--enable-gpu-rasterization` (without it headless Chromium rasterises in software and every page looks slow).
- Altimeter (`.ds-alti`, ≥ 900 px): log scale 1000 km → 50 m near the right edge, platforms ticked, accent diamond
  + value follows the camera; the active chapter (`.is-active`) and its platform tick are emphasised.
- `html.ds-on` (set by backdrop.js unless reduced motion) gives each chapter a screen; `html.orbit-live` while the
  section is on screen (stronger background, mask keeps it off the text column). Phones: the scene centre moves
  below the text. Reduced motion / no JS: the chapters as a plain list.
- Freiburg is marked with the site's diamond (`--accent-mark`, as in the hero and the altimeter); the owner
  disliked the earlier crosshair.
- Labels never overlap the altimeter (`label()` guard). The figure numbers (frame size, overlap, swath, track angle)
  are illustrative of typical surveys; the chapter spec lines (~705 km · 30 m, ~1 km · 20 cm, ~100 m · ~2 cm) are the
  reference.
- Data: `assets/topo/earth.json` (47 KB) from `python3 tools/orbit/build_earth.py <folder with Natural Earth 1:50m
  shapefiles>` (coastline + all admin-0 land borders; only coastline pieces under 1.2° dropped; multi-part lines are split). Credit in the footer ("Natural Earth").

Rejected along the way (don't bring back): an altitude slider explorer, a "Light and water" sun/flood map, a pinned

## Finale: the Feldberg as a colored point cloud (final, 2026-10-13)

The page opens on an old folded paper map (hero) and ends on today's geodata (owner's idea: "show how far the
advancement has come"). `assets/js/finale.js` (WebGL, own canvases `.finale` above the flight, masked off the
Contact text on wide screens) is called by backdrop.js after every frame with its camera (`window.flightFinale`),
so the points sit exactly on the ridge lines. Contact has a long empty top padding (`#contact`, 80vh desktop /
70vh phones) as the finale's stage; the finale's progress (`cam.fin` = k, eased with the camera) runs from
Contact's top at 10 % of the screen (plates gone) to the page end. Sequence (owner's picks, 2026-10-13):

- k 0-0.4: the sand comes back (as the hero's paper blew away): grains in `--contour` drift in from the top left
  and settle into a **flat colored map** (orthophoto colors, 8 m jittered points).
- k 0.4-0.8: the map **rises** into the mountain (the hero's rise, mirrored), while the flight's ridge lines fade
  to 8 % (`FIN_LINES` in backdrop.js: the owner found the brown lines did not go with the cloud).
- k 0.4-0.95: **today's sun** crosses it: the real sun path at the Feldberg for the visitor's date (declination
  and equation of time from the day of year, CET/CEST), from 45 min after sunrise to 45 min before sunset. Slope
  shading (normals with relief x2), **cast shadows** marched through the DEM in the vertex shader (DEM as a 16-bit
  RGBA texture; without vertex texture fetch: no shadows), warm light while the sun is low, slightly cool shade.
- Label above the cloud in open sky, no boxes (they read as white patches): "FELDBERG 1,493 M" (12 px ink),
  "POINT CLOUD · COLORED FROM ORTHOPHOTO" (11 px muted; dropped on phones), "SUNLIGHT · 13 OCT · 17:40" (11 px ink;
  DE "SONNENSTAND · 13. OKT."; never "SUN": the owner read it as Sunday). The owner found 9-10 px unreadable.
- Footprint: **flight strips** across the heading (`STRIP_W` 280 m) with staggered ends, inside a 2.8 km
  radius centred 1 km beyond the summit. **LiDAR edge** (owner's idea, 2026-10-13): beyond each strip's end the
  last scan lines run on as rows of single, smaller points (`FRINGE` 8 lines, `SCAN` 22 m apart, `SCAN_DL` 2.5 m),
  each shorter and sparser than the one before, broken off at random; also along the far strip's open side and,
  half as long (footer line), the near strip's. At 9 m apart the rows blurred back into the edge. Eye-dome lighting (`EDL` 160) and single-pixel hole filling in the EDL
  pass, so paper never shows through as white specks.
- Camera: during the finale backdrop.js moves 1.5 km closer, 300 m lower, lens x1.38 and lifts the view
  (`FIN_*`; the lens, not a closer camera, so the whole cloud stays in view, above the footer line, with sky for
  the label). Centred layouts < 900 px: only a quarter of the step.
- Rejected (2026-10-13, don't bring back): the soft alpha-faded rim (white haze around the cloud), the paper boxes
  behind the label, a round dithered edge, colored ridge lines instead of a cloud (forest reads black), a drone
  flying the strips with a laser fan (owner disliked it), the grey-then-colored ring, a turntable rotation
  (strip ends turned into "fingers"), moving the camera 3 km closer (cloud over the footer).

During the arrival the flight's relief eases to EX x 0.55 (`ARRIVE_RELIEF`, ~1.4x real) and backdrop.js hides its
own summit labels (`flightFinaleOn`). Data from `tools/finale/build_feldberg.py`: `assets/topo/feldberg-dop.webp`
(LGL-BW DOP20 via WMS, 2048 px for 7 km, ~950 KB), `feldberg-dem.bin` (Copernicus GLO-30 warped to 25 m),

## Design rules

The look is "technical survey sheet", deliberately toned down: the cartographic character lives in the hero map,
the mono labels and the flight background. Everything else is quiet. The user prefers calm and centred over decorative.

| `--paper` | `#e9eae6` | `#0e1215` | background |
| `--ink` | `#141719` | `#e4e6e7` | text, work markers |

  Any new token must be defined in all three places (`:root`, the media query, `[data-theme="dark"]`).

### Typography

- IBM Plex Sans (text, headings) and IBM Plex Mono (numbers, dates, coordinates, labels, metadata).
  Self-hosted latin-subset woff2 in `assets/fonts/`. **Only weights 400 and 500.** No other fonts. One exception:
  Reenie Beanie (SIL OFL) for all pencil in the hero (sentence, place names, notes), as SVG outlines built by
  pencil_labels.py, so no font file is loaded for it. Second exception: IBM Plex Serif 500 for the hero sheet's
  margin labels only (sheet number, longitudes, CRS), the old engraved style, in sepia map ink (owner, 2026-10-10).
- Hero `h1`: the owner's sentence "Hello, I am Bakar, a geospatial professional with a passion for all things
  spatial." (DE: "Hallo, ich bin Bakar, Geoexperte mit Leidenschaft für alles Räumliche."; owner dropped "data",
  2026-10-10). No big title, no red coordinates line (owner's wish). It sits on the map just above Freiburg
  (owner, 2026-10-10: "a better place" than bottom-left). It is **drawn in the pencil SVG exactly like the place
  names** (`SAY` in pencil_labels.py: text from the page's `<h1>`, wrapped, centred above Freiburg; phones get
  their own copy `.pc-narrow` inside their cropped view); the real `<h1>` stays in the page, visually hidden, for
  search engines and screen readers. The pencil layer sits above the fold-wear layer (pencil is written after the
  folds: creases shade it but do not rub it off). Arrows are light freehand strokes (`.pc-arrow`). The graphite
  grain is a static noise mask on `.pencil`: a live SVG filter (feTurbulence + displacement) on the pencil layer
  cost ~20 ms per frame while the map rose (owner noticed the scroll stutter, 2026-10-10). No live SVG filters
  on large hero layers.
  As live text in a web font it rendered visibly thicker than the outlined place names; the owner wants them the
  same. It is **pencilled on the sheet**
  in Reenie Beanie, a pencil scrawl (owner: "perfect", 2026-10-10, picked from 7 hands; Caveat looked bold), in
  blue pencil (`--pencil-blue`; owner picked it over red, purple and graphite; only the sentence is blue, the rest
  stays graphite: two colours at most) at the place names' size (130 m on the map scale), tilted −1.2°, with the same graphite
  filter and fold wear. Light graphite: rendered contrast on the aged paper is low (~3:1); the owner chose the
  light pencil over a darker "pressed harder" version. It fades out with the paper as the map rises. Seen and rejected: Plex
  Serif, serif italic, letterpress, an embossed seal, Caveat, Kalam, Architects Daughter and other hands. It fades out and lifts as the map starts to rise
  (with the paper, see below).
- Running text is 15px (0.9375rem) everywhere, one size per text block (no bigger "lead" paragraphs); the owner
  found the earlier scale too big.
- Section titles: Plex Sans 500, `clamp(1.625rem, …, 2.375rem)`, `letter-spacing: -0.035em`. No `[0n]` numbers above
  them or in the nav (owner removed them, 2026-10-09).
- Mono is for metadata only: never for running text.
- Centre only short text (headings, one-liners, at most about 3 lines; use `text-wrap: balance`). Longer text is
  left-aligned, even inside centred columns.
